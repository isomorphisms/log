# Gratitude

The number of people who deserve things and are going to get, you know, their big thank-you note is going to be, "Hey, I used your software without paying you"—that number of people is extremely vast.

## Andy Chu and Oil

Starting with Andy Chu, the developer of the Oil shell, who spent easily a decade thinking difficult thoughts about what shells really are, what they should be, how they could be better, how they could be simpler, what it is that makes Bash Bash, what it is that we like about Ruby or Python that makes us prefer JavaScript, Ruby, or Python to Bash, and even raising a grant to pay a C++ developer to make this shell fast.

I paid a couple bucks to a data center and had a computer model rewrite the Oil shell in D, right after I had the same bot create a bunch of different data types and work on back-end compilers that can—so I'm told, although the testing has not gone much further than between my apartment and some cluster of data centers—pack certain data types more efficiently or compute two- and three-dimensional rotations correctly at the register level. And much more is to come.

That's to say nothing of the type unification, where all I've really had to do is mention books that I've read in my life and basically ask the computer model to figure out what to do with it.

## Three times programming felt exciting

I don't really have an issue with the vibe-coding era.

There are only a couple moments that I really felt excited by programming during, let's say, the last decade.

The first was when I started playing around with Verilog.

The second was when I started using AutoHotkey, because even if I messaged the developers of this stuff we were using at work and asked them to open up an API so I could curl things in and make my paperwork faster, it wasn't going to happen, even though it would have been good for the company. So AutoHotkey was a win for my team.

And then the third is the era of vibe coding.

## A note to programmers

If your job has been taken away, just remember that Chinese people are being enslaved and this is how you're able to afford things. It's not because of technology; it's because of American jobs being replaced with cheaper labor.

My friend was working at a bridal shop and opened up the box containing the dress, and what else was inside besides the dress? A slip of paper saying: help, we are being kept prisoner and forced to work.

Programmers had it good for a very long time, and be happy that you did. Unless you've been working in a cobalt mine, your lot is not the worst of everyone's.

I don't think all of us are going to become electricians either. Just a friendly word of warning.

That said, I guarantee you this is not the end of your life. It's the end of a chapter of your life.

Along with all the bad feelings is going to come some personal change, and if you've given yourself so much over to your career that you can barely understand yourself without thinking about this work, then a form of self-discovery that you have been doing without for a long time is on the horizon.

I know that these statements are not going to help you feel better.
